    How I work

    The same sequence whether it's a new surface or the third iteration of an existing one. Half of it happens outside the design tool, and that half decides whether the other half lands.

    1. 01

      Learn the domain

      Start with how the work actually gets done: who does it, under what conditions, what a mistake costs them. In a technical product the user knows the domain better than I do.

    2. 02

      Find what moves

      The request is rarely the problem. I look for what would actually move — adoption, build cost, time to a decision — and design against that.

    3. 03

      Shape the product

      Decide what gets built before deciding how it looks: scope, sequencing, the flows that carry the value, and what stays out of this release.

    4. 04

      Design the detail

      States, edge cases, error paths, content variability. What engineering gets is unambiguous to build, not just convincing in the happy path.

    5. 05

      Build the architecture

      One foundation, many outputs: variables, tokens, patterns. This is what decides whether the next feature costs as much as the last one.

    6. 06

      Ship, then measure

      Next to engineering until it's live, then watch what it does and iterate. Adoption and documentation are part of the design, not after it.

    Twice in the last two years, phase 02 produced work nobody had asked for — and it turned out to be the most valuable thing that shipped.
